## 6. Existing online-presence assessment

- Google Maps listing only. No website, no social profiles discoverable, no food-delivery presence discoverable. The photographed price board is the café's current public "menu system" — a website that digitises exactly that board (with its date caveat) is a real service to potential visitors.

## 7. Opportunities for the website (all grounded in verified facts)

1. Lead with the **₹10 chai** — a verified price is the strongest hook a student café has.
2. Publish the **photographed price board as a proper menu** (with the 2021 date caveat + call CTA).
3. **"PURE VEG"** as a first-class fact (it is on their sign).
4. Location clarity: "opposite Government Engineering College" + directions to the exact listing.
5. Wheelchair-accessible seating stated in the visit section (from the About tab).

## 8. Unverified facts intentionally EXCLUDED from the website

- Any menu item or price beyond the seven photographed entries; any combination meals, offers, or combos.
- Review count and review quotes.
- Founding date, owner name, history, awards, Wi-Fi claims, "best chai in Bilaspur" style claims.
- Instagram or any social handle.
- Any food photograph other than the café's own listing photo — **no stock food photography is used anywhere on this site**, because stock food would misrepresent a specific real kitchen.

## 9. Creative direction (grounded in the evidence above)

**"The college adda price board."** The real signage is loud red 3D lettering with a hand-written "cafe" script, on an open-air shack across the road from an engineering college, with a ₹10 tea board. The design leans into that vernacular-poster honesty instead of fighting it: signal red, cream, and ink; Anton (a billboard condensed sans that mirrors the 3D letters) paired with Hanken Grotesk; real prices set as display typography; the actual photographed board presented as an artefact; Hindi name बिग बॉस कैफे used as a typographic texture; sharp hard-edged cards, not soft rounded glass. Where Green House Cafe (the sibling project) is an evening garden, Big Boss is a bright morning board — the two designs share nothing visually on purpose.
